Common Questions

Personal Injury FAQ

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim?
Statutes of limitations vary by state — typically between 1 and 3 years from the date of injury. Some exceptions apply, such as cases involving minors or injuries that weren't immediately apparent. Contact us as soon as possible to protect your rights.
What if I was partially at fault for the accident?
Most states follow comparative negligence rules, meaning you can still recover compensation even if you were partially at fault — it's simply reduced by your percentage of fault. We'll give you an honest assessment of how fault may affect your specific claim.
How much is my case worth?
Compensation can include medical expenses (past and future), lost wages and lost earning capacity, pain and suffering, property damage, and loss of enjoyment of life. The value depends heavily on the severity of your injuries, the strength of the liability evidence, and the insurance coverage available.
What if the insurance company already made me an offer?
Do not accept any settlement offer before speaking with an attorney. Initial offers from insurance companies are almost always far below what a case is actually worth. Once you accept a settlement, you permanently waive your right to seek additional compensation — even if your injuries worsen.
Do I have to go to court?
The majority of personal injury cases settle before trial. However, we prepare every case as if it will go to court — which is exactly what forces insurance companies to negotiate seriously. If trial is necessary to get you a fair outcome, we're fully prepared to take it there.
